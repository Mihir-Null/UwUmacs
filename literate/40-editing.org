chapter configures it and hands =SPC= to [[file:41-leader.org][the UwUmacs leader]].

* How Meow thinks

Meow is modal, like Vim, but the order is reversed. In Vim you say *what to do*
and then *what to do it to* (=d w=, delete a word). In Meow you first *select*
something, extend the selection if you like, and then *act* on it (=w= marks
a word, =d= deletes the selection). Because the selection is visible before
you act, you always see what a command will touch, and Meow can print little
hints on screen while you extend with =e= or a digit.

Meow has a few *states*, shown in the mode line:

- *Normal*: keys select and act. This is where you live.
- *Insert*: keys type text. =i=, =a=, =c= and =A=/=I= enter it; =ESC= leaves.
- *Motion*: for read-only application buffers such as Dired, Magit and Help.
  The buffer keeps its own keys; only =j=/=k= and the leader are added.
- *Beacon*: Meow's multiple-cursor mode. Unchanged here; see =M-x meow-tutor=.

Run =M-x meow-tutor= once. It teaches the grammar interactively in an hour.

* The grammar

Meow ships no default Normal-state keys; every configuration defines its own.
This one keeps the layout Meow's own documentation and =meow-tutor= describe,
so upstream help transfers. The lower-case key moves or selects, and the
shifted key does the same while *extending* the current selection. Digits
pick one of the numbered expansion hints Meow draws after a movement.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(defun starter-meow-setup ()
  "Install the QWERTY selection grammar and the Motion-state basics."
  ;; Motion state: application buffers keep their own keys; only j/k move.
  (meow-motion-define-key
   '("j" . meow-next)
   '("k" . meow-prev))
  (meow-normal-define-key
   ;; Numbered expansion hints and counts.
   '("0" . meow-expand-0)
   '("9" . meow-expand-9)
   '("8" . meow-expand-8)
   '("7" . meow-expand-7)
   '("6" . meow-expand-6)
   '("5" . meow-expand-5)
   '("4" . meow-expand-4)
   '("3" . meow-expand-3)
   '("2" . meow-expand-2)
   '("1" . meow-expand-1)
   '("-" . negative-argument)
   ;; Move; the shifted key extends the selection instead.
   '("h" . meow-left)
   '("H" . meow-left-expand)
   '("j" . meow-next)
   '("J" . meow-next-expand)
   '("k" . meow-prev)
   '("K" . meow-prev-expand)
   '("l" . meow-right)
   '("L" . meow-right-expand)
   '("b" . meow-back-word)
   '("B" . meow-back-symbol)
   '("e" . meow-next-word)
   '("E" . meow-next-symbol)
   '("g" . beginning-of-buffer)
   '("G" . end-of-buffer)
   ;; Select a thing: word, symbol, line, block, or by character.
   '("w" . meow-mark-word)
   '("W" . meow-mark-symbol)
   '("x" . meow-line)
   '("o" . meow-block)
   '("O" . meow-to-block)

* Things, starting states and package shims

A *thing* is what =,= and =.= select: a word, a pair of brackets, a string.
Angle brackets are added to the built-in set. =meow-mode-state-list= decides
the state a buffer starts in; terminals start in Insert because their keys
belong to the shell, and Magit starts in Normal so its own keys and the
selection grammar coexist. Once everything is set, Meow is enabled globally
and the leader is attached.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(use-package meow
  :ensure t
  :custom
  (meow-use-cursor-position-hack t)
  (meow-use-clipboard t)
  (meow-goto-line-function #'consult-goto-line)
  :config
  (setopt meow-use-dynamic-face-color nil)
  (meow-thing-register 'angle '(regexp "<" ">") '(regexp "<" ">"))
  (add-to-list 'meow-char-thing-table '(?a . angle))
  (dolist (entry '((magit-status-mode . normal)
                   (magit-log-mode . normal)
                   (eshell-mode . insert)
                   (shell-mode . insert)
                   (term-mode . insert)))
    (add-to-list 'meow-mode-state-list entry))
  (with-eval-after-load 'magit
    (add-to-list 'meow-grab-fill-commands 'magit-discard)
    (add-hook 'magit-mode-hook
              (lambda ()
                (local-unset-key (kbd "j"))
                (local-unset-key (kbd "k")))))
  (with-eval-after-load 'org
    ;; Treat @ as part of symbols/words during Meow movement in Org.
    (modify-syntax-entry ?@ "_" org-mode-syntax-table))
  (starter-meow-setup)
  (meow-global-mode 1)
  (uwumacs-leader-enable))

#+end_src

group: =SPC SPC= runs a command by name, =SPC /= describes every leader key
that works in the current buffer, and the rest are quick single-key actions.
The letters not listed here are groups, explained next.

* Temporary bridge to Lambda's groups

Lambda's keybinding module still defines the group maps (=SPC f= files,
=SPC b= buffers, =SPC s= search, and so on) as children of =lem+leader-map=.
Until the keys chapter owns the whole tree, the UwUmacs leader *inherits*
from that map: a key not found in the leader falls through to Lambda's map.
Two adjustments make the inheritance safe. Lambda put a mail menu on =m=,
which is the localleader key, so that entry is cleared. And the modules that
still add keys with =meow-leader-define-key= are pointed at the UwUmacs map,
so every writer lands in one place. The non-modal prefix =C-c C-SPC= opens the
same map, which is how you reach the leader from Insert state.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(with-eval-after-load 'lem-setup-keybindings
  (define-key lem+leader-map (kbd "m") nil)
  (set-keymap-parent uwumacs-leader-map lem+leader-map)
  (setf (alist-get 'leader meow-keymap-alist) uwumacs-leader-map)
  (keymap-global-set lem-prefix uwumacs-leader-map)
  (keymap-set uwumacs-leader-map "p" (cons "project" project-prefix-map))
  (keymap-set uwumacs-leader-map "v" (cons "version control" lem+vc-keys)))

(provide 'starter-setup-meow)
;;; starter-setup-meow.el ends here
#+end_src
